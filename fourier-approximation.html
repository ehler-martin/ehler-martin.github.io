Numerical harmonic analysis

Quantitative estimates: How well does the discrete Fourier transform approximate the Fourier transform on ℝ?

Suppose we can choose n equally spaced values of a function on ℝ. How far apart should we place them to approximate its Fourier transform? The answer depends on how quickly the function and its transform each decay.

Martin Ehler, Karlheinz Gröchenig & Andreas Klotz

SIAM Review 68 (1), 93–123 · 2026

Read the paper Open preprint

How the decay determines the grid

With n points spaced h apart, the sampling interval has length p = nh. A larger spacing lets the grid reach farther along the real line. A smaller spacing resolves finer variations in the function.

Theorem 2.1 bounds the approximation error by the sum of two terms. The first depends on the decay of f and gets smaller as p grows. The second depends on the decay of its Fourier transform f̂ and gets smaller as h shrinks.

E ≤ C (p−α + hβ)

For the function in the figure, f decays more slowly than f̂. Balancing the two terms therefore gives more of the grid to the extent of the interval. The paper compares the usual step size h = n−1/2 with h = n−3/10. The latter gives a longer interval at the same sample count.

At 4,096 samples, the adapted grid gives an error of about 3.4 × 10−5. The standard grid takes about 131,072 samples to reach a comparable error. Both curves use exactly the same function and error measure.

A function built to test the estimate

To test a rate of convergence, one needs a function whose decay is known on both sides of the Fourier transform. One also needs an independent way to evaluate the transform and measure the actual error.

The authors construct such functions from translates of a B-spline, a piecewise polynomial that vanishes outside a finite interval. In f2,4, the coefficients give quadratic decay in x. The cubic B-spline gives fourth-order decay in ξ. The superscripts record those two orders.

The error norm and the convergence exponents

At each grid point k/p, compare the exact value f̂(k/p) with the scaled discrete Fourier transform of the values f(jh). The error E in equation (2.2) is the square root of the sum of the squared absolute differences divided by p. The indices satisfy −n/2 < j, k ≤ n/2.

If f and f̂ are continuous and have polynomial decay of orders a, b > 1, Theorem 2.1 allows any 0 < α < a − 1/2 and 0 < β < b − 1/2. Balancing the terms gives a step size proportional to n−α/(α+β) and an error of order at most n−αβ/(α+β).

For f2,4, the limiting values are α = 3/2 and β = 7/2. They give the reference rates n−3/4 for the standard grid and n−21/20 for the adapted grid. The computed errors follow these slopes. The theorem proves rates arbitrarily close to the limiting exponents. Read Theorem 2.1.
